Keeping Better Reverse Engineering Notes

November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

A disassembly view can feel self-explanatory while it is open in front of you. A week later, the same function may look unfamiliar. Notes bridge that gap.

A useful note structure

  1. Context: binary, version, architecture, and analysis objective.
  2. Observation: what the code demonstrably does.
  3. Evidence: function names, addresses, call sites, and screenshots where useful.
  4. Hypothesis: what the behavior might imply, clearly separated from fact.
  5. Next step: the smallest experiment that can confirm or reject the hypothesis.

Prefer precise statements

Instead of writing “this function decrypts the payload,” record the observable operations first: input buffer, transformation, output buffer, and callers. Use “appears to decrypt” until the evidence is sufficient.

A good notebook should let another analyst follow your reasoning without having to repeat every step.
